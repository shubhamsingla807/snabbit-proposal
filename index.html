06 · Drilling down 5 Whys

Why is repeat rate weak?

Why 1Users book once, then drop off, even after a successful experience.
Why 2There's no anchor pulling them back. They have to actively remember and re-decide each time.
Why 3Snabbit is sold as a transactional service ("book in 10 min"), not as an embedded household routine.
Why 4The product has no habit-forming layer: no recurring slot, no "your usual" cleaner, no weekly cadence baked into the app.
Why 5The company won the acquisition wedge ("10-min delivery") but hasn't built the retention engine yet. Each transaction is treated as a fresh sale.
07 · Root cause In one sentence

The unit of commerce is wrong.

Root cause
Snabbit's unit of commerce is the booking. It should be the household relationship.

Everything downstream — pricing, retention, supply matching, even support — flows from this one decision. Fix the unit, fix the math.

11 · The solution Three layers, sequenced

Hygiene first. Subscription on top. Experiments inside.

The mistake is bundling these as one launch. They're three different bets with different owners, timelines, and risk profiles.

Layer 1 · Hygiene

Table stakes for everyone

  • "Your usual" cleaner matching
  • Failure guarantee + auto-refund

Lifts baseline retention. Substrate for Layer 2.

Layer 2 · Growth bet

The subscription product

  • Recurring slot (flexible cadence)
  • "Your usual" guaranteed
  • Failure guarantee enhanced

The new SKU. The single biggest lever.

Layer 3 · Tests inside L2

Experiments, not features

  • Pricing tier test (annual vs monthly)
  • Pitch timing test (when to offer)

Run after L2 ships. A/B, not bundled.

12 · Layer 1 detail Hygiene · Ship for everyone

Two things that lift the baseline.

Your usual cleaner

What: Right after the user rates their first service, ask one question: "Liked them? Keep same as next time?" If yes, the next booking auto-prefers that worker. If unavailable at slot time, show next-best match with an "alternate" tag.

Why: The biggest drop happens between booking 1 and booking 2. A "stranger every time" bar is high. Locking in the same person right after a positive moment likely lifts second-time bookings the most.

Cost: Matching logic + a single rating-flow question + UI tag. Light supply ops impact.

Failure guarantee

What: In-app "service is going wrong" flow. Two taps to flag, auto-issued partial refund, replacement worker dispatched, supervisor follow-up. Visible upfront on the booking screen, not buried in T&Cs.

Why: First-time users carry the most risk anxiety. Surfacing the guarantee before the first booking lifts conversion. Existing users get a real recovery path instead of the 24-hour WhatsApp queue.

Cost: Refund automation + escalation rules. Saves CX hours and stops the 1-star review spiral.

13 · Layer 2 The subscription product

Snabbit Plus.
Your home, on autopilot.

A new SKU built around flexible recurring service. Users pick the cadence that fits their life. The household, not the booking, becomes the unit of commerce.

Weekly Biweekly Twice a week Pick a specific day Custom dates

A subscriber gets guaranteed "your usual" cleaner, an enhanced failure guarantee (priority replacement, full refund), and the convenience of never having to remember to book. The transactional unlock becomes a relationship.

14 · Layer 3 Experiments inside the subscription

Two structured A/B tests after launch.

These aren't features to ship. They're levers to test once Layer 2 is live.

A

Pricing tier test

Hypothesis: Power users (≥4 services completed in past 60 days) have higher willingness to pay and lock in longer plans.

Design: Two arms — flat ₹X/mo for all vs intent-segmented (annual default for power users, monthly for new).

Read: Subscription attach × ARPU per arm. Run 4 weeks.

B

Pitch timing test

Hypothesis: Pitching after the 2nd successful booking outperforms Day 1 onboarding pitch (less skepticism, more lived value).

Design: Three arms — Day 1, after 1st booking, after 2nd booking.

Read: Subscription attach + downstream W4 retention.

15 · Risks What I'd watch

Three honest risks.

Supply commitment

"Your usual" depends on cleaners staying on platform and in the user's pincode. Churn on the supply side breaks the promise. Need a worker-retention story alongside.

Refund abuse

Failure guarantee is gameable. Mitigate with rate limits, photo evidence prompts, and a ladder (partial → full → ban after N abuses).

Cannibalization

If subscribers consume more services per ₹ than transactional users pay, margin tightens. Need to model the unit economics before scaling beyond pilot.
